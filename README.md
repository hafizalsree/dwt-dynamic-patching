# Abstract
Patch-based transformers tokenise a time series into fixed-size, uniform windows, which commits the model to a single temporal resolution even though real signals are multi-scale. The discrete wavelet transform (DWT) is a natural alternative because it decomposes a signal across scales. A growing body of work makes the wavelet filter learnable, on the assumption that a more flexible wavelet forecasts better. That assumption has rarely been tested in isolation, because a single reported improvement usually credits three things at once. These are the use of any multi-resolution decomposition, the choice of wavelet and training the filter. We introduce a forecaster that turns a learnable-DWT per-scale energy map into variable-length patches by merging adjacent windows that share a dominant scale. We use it to run within-model ablations that vary one factor at a time. Three findings follow. First, the multi-resolution decomposition itself accounts for most of the improvement. A frozen wavelet, with no learning at all, beats a fixed-patch baseline on eight of the nine datasets by 1.5% to 7.8%, and on four of them it recovers all of the gain. Traffic is the one exception, where the decomposition itself is 1.2% worse. Second, learning the filter gives a further improvement on only some datasets. It helps on three of the nine datasets and makes Traffic 2.7% worse. Two of the three winners are finely sampled series whose high-pass band reshapes over the first few epochs and then settles, but hourly Electricity gains the most of all and none of the measures we examined accounts for it. Third, having variable-length patches helps, but exactly where their boundaries fall does not. A fixed-size uniform grid, even with its patch size tuned per task, is worse than our variable-length patches on 26 of 36 settings. Recomputing the boundaries from the learned filter at every step, rather than caching them once, never helps. The improvement in wavelet-based forecasting comes from the multi-resolution structure and the variable-length patches it induces, not from making the wavelet learnable. In practice, start from any valid wavelet, cache the patch boundaries once and treat training the filter as a per-dataset decision rather than a default.
